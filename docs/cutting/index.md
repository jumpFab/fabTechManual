# Milling

## What is Milling?

Milling is the process of removing material using rotary cutters. In fabrication, this means using CNC (Computer Numerical Control) machines to carve precision parts out of solid blocks of wood, metal, or plastic.

Milling is the cornerstone of high-precision engineering, allowing for the creation of functional mechanical parts and circuit boards with tight tolerances.

!!! example "In Practice"
    An engineer designs a motor mount, which the CNC mill creates by spinning a carbide bit at high speeds to shave away excess aluminum from a raw block.

---

## Key Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Spindle** | The rotating axis of the machine that holds and spins the cutting tool. |
| **Feed Rate** | The speed at which the cutting tool moves through the material. |
| **End Mill** | A type of industrial cutting bit used in milling applications. |
| **Workholding** | Any device (like a vise or clamps) used to keep the material stationary during cutting. |

---

!!! danger "Category-Wide Hazards"
    All Milling machines share these risks:

    - **Projectile Debris:** High-speed cutters generate "chips" that can cause eye injuries or skin punctures.
    - **Entanglement:** Loose clothing or hair can be pulled into the high-torque rotating spindle.
    - **Sharp Tooling:** End mills are extremely sharp even when stationary and can cause deep lacerations.

---

## How It Works

Milling uses a rotating cutting tool that moves along multiple axes (\( X \), \( Y \), and \( Z \)). As the sharp edges of the tool impact the workpiece, they mechanically shear off small chips of material until the desired shape is achieved.
