# Formative

## What is Formative?

Formative is the process of shaping material by applying pressure or using a mold. In fabrication, this typically involves vacuum forming, injection molding, or heat bending.

Formative processes are essential for moving from a single prototype to "batch production," allowing for the rapid duplication of shapes from a master mold.

!!! example "In Practice"
    A designer uses a wooden mold of a mask, which the vacuum former covers with a heated plastic sheet, sucking it tight against the mold to copy its shape.

---

## Key Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Draft Angle** | The taper given to a mold to allow the formed part to be easily removed. |
| **Thermoplastic** | A plastic material that becomes pliable or moldable above a specific temperature. |
| **Undercut** | A feature of a part that prevents it from being pulled straight out of a mold. |
| **Thermoforming** | The process of heating a plastic sheet until it is soft enough to be shaped. |

---

!!! danger "Category-Wide Hazards"
    All Formative machines share these risks:

    - **Extreme Heat:** Large heating elements stay hot long after the machine is turned off.
    - **Pinch/Crush Hazards:** Hydraulic presses and vacuum tables exert hundreds of pounds of force.
    - **Off-gassing:** Overheating plastics can release hazardous fumes (e.g., Chlorine gas from PVC).

---

## How It Works

Formative manufacturing reshapes material without adding or removing it. This is usually achieved by bringing a material to its "Glass Transition Temperature" and using vacuum, air pressure, or physical plates to force it into a specific geometry.
