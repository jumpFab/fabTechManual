# Laser

## What is Laser?

Laser (Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation) is the process of using a concentrated beam of light to cut, engrave, or mark materials. In fabrication, this means using high-power \( CO_2 \) or Fiber lasers for rapid prototyping.

Laser cutters are often the most used machines in a Fab Lab due to their speed, precision, and ability to work with a wide variety of materials.

!!! example "In Practice"
    A user designs a flat-pack box, which the laser cutter produces by burning through a sheet of plywood along a designated vector path.

---

## Key Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Rastering** | An engraving process where the laser moves back and forth like an inkjet printer to etch images. |
| **Vectoring** | A cutting process where the laser follows a continuous path to cut through material. |
| **Frequency** | The number of laser pulses per second; crucial for different material finishes. |
| **Focal Point** | The height at which the laser beam is most concentrated for maximum cutting power. |

!!! danger "Category-Wide Hazards"
    All Laser machines share these risks:

    - **Fire:** The laser works by burning material; if the beam dwells too long, the workpiece can ignite.
    - **Invisible Radiation:** Reflections of the beam can cause permanent blindness. Never bypass safety interlocks.
    - **Toxic Air Contaminants:** Laser-cutting materials like Acrylic or MDF releases carcinogenic smoke.

---

## How It Works

A laser machine generates a high-energy beam that is directed through a series of mirrors and a focusing lens. The intense heat at the focal point vaporizes or melts the material. The process is assisted by a stream of compressed air (Air Assist) to blow away debris and prevent combustion.
