# Assembly

## What is Assembly?

Assembly is the process of joining components together to form a complete system or product. In fabrication, this means using fasteners, adhesives, or soldering to combine parts created by other machines.

Assembly is where separate components become functional prototypes. It requires a mix of manual dexterity and specialized joining tools.

!!! example "In Practice"
    A maker assembles a robot by soldering electronic components onto a PCB and fastening the board to a 3D-printed chassis using M3 screws.

---

## Key Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Soldering** | Joining metal items by flowing a filler metal (solder) into the joint. |
| **Tolerance** | The allowable amount of variation in the dimensions of parts to ensure they fit together. |
| **Fasteners** | Hardware like screws, bolts, and rivets used to mechanically join parts. |
| **Adhesive** | Substances like epoxy or cyanoacrylate used to bond surfaces together. |

---

!!! danger "Category-Wide Hazards"
    All Assembly processes share these risks:

    - **Fumes:** Soldering and chemical adhesives release toxic vapors that require ventilation.
    - **Thermal Burns:** Soldering irons and heat guns reach temperatures high enough to damage skin.
    - **Eye Strain:** Fine assembly work can lead to fatigue; proper lighting and magnification are required.

---

## How It Works

Assembly involves the mechanical or chemical bonding of materials. It relies on "Fit and Finish," where the precision of previously manufactured parts determines how easily the final object can be constructed.
